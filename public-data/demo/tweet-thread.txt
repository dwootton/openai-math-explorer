I made a map of OpenAI’s math repo: 372 research families, 722 manuscripts. Click a point to read the paper, see related work, or open its linked Lean material.

Here’s a demo.

2/7
Click a subject label to dim everything else. The selection stays put while you browse.

You can also compare papers by mathematical objects, proof techniques, or a list of concepts you write yourself. Each choice gives you different neighbors.

3/7
For example, the map links two distance problems: Falconer’s conjecture concerns fractal sets; Erdős-style problems count distances between finite points.

There’s a useful connection, but the assumptions and conclusions are different.

4/7
ELI5 gives you context, definitions, and references from the manuscript. Switch it off to read the original claim. The app remembers your choice.

These are reading aids. They don’t establish that a paper’s proof is correct.

5/7
The embeddings suggest six broad clusters, with weak separation. About 58% of a family’s five nearest neighbors share its subject label.

I also tried a newer model. It changed the map, but tied the old model on a small search check: 9/12 first-place matches.

6/7
One thing to watch: 137 of the 372 families have no linked Lean material in this snapshot. The other 235 have links, but those may cover only part of a family.

That’s the repo’s coverage, not a broken link in the explorer.
