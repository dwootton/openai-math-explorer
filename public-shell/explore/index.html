How references are compared

Families are compared by the works listed in their manuscripts’ bibliographies. DOI and arXiv aliases are merged, with conservative title-and-author matching for other citations. Each work counts once per family. Widely shared works count less, long lists are normalized, and scores based on very few shared works are reduced.

Open a neighbor’s shared works to see the evidence. This measures shared literature, not whether two proofs use a citation in the same way. Unmatched families sit in a separate row. Map positions are approximate; lines follow the reference ranking.

What the Lean comparison uses

Topics embeds family titles and descriptions. Lean proofs embeds named Lean targets and two levels of statically resolved supporting declarations. Both use the same pinned EmbeddingGemma 2 model, SentenceSimilarity task, 1,024-token chunks with 96-token overlap, and a normalized average of unique chunk embeddings. Neighbors use cosine similarity; there is no prompt projection.

Expansion follows unambiguous source names within the imported modules we indexed. It is not compiler-verified: implicit instances, macros, ambiguous or short unqualified references, uncached imports and external libraries such as Mathlib are not expanded. This is partial proof-source coverage, not the full elaborated proof or proof verification. Families without linked Lean solutions are not plotted or ranked. Topics compares 372 families; Lean compares 235, using 403 of 405 linked entries. Two import-only entries with unresolved targets are excluded. Compare ranks within the same eligible group when contrasting the two views.
